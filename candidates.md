# Line selection — how the entry was chosen

Judge brief (from the kit): deadpan robot voice first, then a real laugh,
then surprise / a specific detail, then tightness with the punch at the end.
Length for its own sake does not count.

## Candidates tested (all ≤ 200 characters)

| # | Chars | Line |
|---|-------|------|
| A | 119 | The dust in that corner unionised at 06:00. I am in active negotiations and, per protocol, I do not cross picket lines. |
| B | 83 | That crumb has been there since Tuesday. We have bonded. I do not clean my friends. |
| C | 103 | I surveyed that spot, found it 12% dustier than regulation, and granted it protected historical status. |
| D | 108 | My lidar classified that spot as 'already clean.' The dust has since filed an appeal, which I have not read. |
| E | 112 | That spot filed a restraining order against my brushes in 2023. I respect court orders, even the ones I made up. |

## Why A won

- **Deadpan machine voice:** "per protocol", "active negotiations", a timestamped
  event log ("at 06:00") — the vacuum reports a labour dispute like a status code.
- **Surprise:** nobody expects the dust to organise. The turn lands in the first
  sentence and the punch ("I do not cross picket lines") closes the line.
- **Specific detail:** 06:00, the corner, negotiations — concrete where B and C
  stay generic.
- **Tightness:** every clause earns its place; 119 characters, no filler words.

B was the runner-up: a good punch ("I do not clean my friends") but a thinner
setup. D and E are solid but read more like a cute observation than a joke that
lands. C leans on bureaucracy without a real laugh.

## Final entry (119 characters)

> The dust in that corner unionised at 06:00. I am in active negotiations and, per protocol, I do not cross picket lines.
